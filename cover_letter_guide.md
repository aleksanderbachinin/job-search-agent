# Cover Letter Guide — rules for every application

Source: Yulia Zimina, "Гайд для создания Cover Letter" (Notion, https://app.notion.com/p/Cover-Letter-2a8b9dffef2f816f8d49cec7c46744fd). Rules below are a working summary of that guide, adapted to Alexander's applications. Follow them for every cover letter in `applications_YYYY-MM-DD/<Company>/`.

## Purpose
- The letter has one job: make the recruiter open the CV. It must work on its own, not as a footnote to the CV.
- Treat it like a short B2B sales email to a busy person: they skim for seconds, and generated-sounding text is obvious.
- Before writing, analyze the company and the vacancy to find their pain points. Then show how Alexander handled similar situations and what the results were.
- Write only when the vacancy is worth the extra 15 minutes.

## Length and shape
- 250–300 words max (half an A4 page). Up to 3 paragraphs of body, no filler.
- Email: subject line → salutation → hook → body → closing + CTA → signature.
- Message (Hirify, Telegram, LinkedIn, form field): same structure without the subject line.

## Punctuation
- Never use em dashes (—) or en dashes (–) anywhere in the letter, subject line included. They are the clearest tell of generated text.
- Use a comma, colon, full stop or brackets instead, or split the sentence in two. A hyphen inside a compound word ("conversion-focused", "sign-up") is fine.

## Structure
1. **Subject line** (email only). Goal: get it opened. Role + years + key strength, e.g. "Senior Product Manager, 9 years in AI & SaaS growth". Never "Job Application" / "<Role> Application".
2. **Salutation.** Find the recruiter's or hiring manager's name on LinkedIn. Fallbacks: "Dear <Company> Hiring Team," (email), "Hello! / Hi <Name>," (message). Never "To Whom It May Concern".
3. **Opening hook (first 2 sentences).** Name the role and immediately connect one specific piece of experience to one specific thing about the company (from their posting, blog, product, news). No "I'm applying for the position…" as a bare statement.
4. **Body (2–3 short paragraphs).** Pick 1–2 key requirements from the vacancy. Back each with a case in STAR/CAR form: situation/task → action → measurable result. Numbers only from the master resume / candidate memory — never invent metrics.
5. **Closing + call to action.** Direct, not pleading: say the experience matches, that the CV is attached, and when Alexander is available to talk ("available for a call next week", "happy to walk you through how I'd approach X").
6. **Signature.** "Best regards," + Alexander Bachinin + email + LinkedIn (+ Telegram).

## Phrases to avoid → what to write instead
| Avoid | Use instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| To Whom It May Concern | Dear Hiring Manager / Dear <Team> Team / Dear <Name> and Team | Shows you checked who you're writing to |
| I'm applying for the position… (and nothing else) | I'm applying for your <Role> role. My experience with <specific thing> fits <their need> | Opens with relevant experience, not a statement of fact |
| I'm a hard worker / team player / results-driven | A concrete achievement with action + result | Clichés waste space; only impact counts |
| I would be perfect for this role | My background in <X> covers the key requirements of <Y> | Confident, not arrogant: a fit on specific points |
| I'm passionate about what you do | I've been following <Company>'s <specific work>… / Your approach to <X> matches my <Y> | Interest backed by facts about the company |
| This position would help me grow | I want to apply <expertise> while expanding into <area> to help with <their goal> | Mutual benefit, not "what I want from you" |
| I think I would be a great fit | My <N years of X> and <Y> match the requirements | Facts, not "I think" (use "think" at most once) |

Ambitions: for lead/senior roles talk about building, influencing, growing the team; for IC roles talk about deeper expertise and hard problems. Don't mention entrepreneurial ambitions (relevant for Alexander: frame co-founder history as product results, not as a wish to start his own company again).

## Pre-send checklist
Structure and style:
- [ ] ≤ 250–300 words
- [ ] None of the phrases from the "avoid" list
- [ ] No em dashes or en dashes anywhere in the letter
- [ ] Specific subject line with experience (email only)
- [ ] Personalized salutation (name if findable)
- [ ] First sentence is a hook tied to this company
- [ ] Achievements in STAR/CAR form
- [ ] Clear numeric results (from master resume only)
- [ ] Focus on value for the company, not on Alexander's wishes
- [ ] Clear call to action ("available for a call…")
- [ ] Contacts + LinkedIn in the signature

Grammar and details:
- [ ] Proofread for grammar (polish, don't regenerate — the letter must sound like Alexander, not like an AI)
- [ ] Company name, manager name and role title spelled exactly as in the posting
- [ ] CV attached as PDF with a professional file name (`Bachinin_Alexander_<Company>_<Role>.pdf`)
